## I build from the substrate

I am a self-taught engineer from Ethiopia working across **machine intelligence, programming languages, operating systems, infrastructure, and human-facing software**.

The projects here are connected by one habit: I do not begin with the most visible interface. I begin underneath it — with the representation, memory model, protocol, runtime, or assumption that determines what the interface can become.

That is why this portfolio moves from Ethiopian-language tokenization to causal computation, from spiking cognition to bare-metal systems, and from browser control planes to school infrastructure. The surface changes. The question stays the same: **what should be rebuilt before the next layer is allowed to exist?**

## A few things I care about

**Foundations over wrappers.** I prefer understanding the mechanism before selecting the abstraction. That leads to blank-slate model training, low-level vision and audio pipelines, custom tokenizers for Amharic and Ge'ez, and software that treats persistence, memory, and execution as design decisions rather than defaults.

**Proof, measurement, and honest scope.** A research prototype is valuable when its claims are testable. A production system is valuable when it remains dependable outside a demonstration. I try to keep those categories clear while moving ideas from mathematical hypothesis to reproducible implementation.

**Technology with a place to land.** The work is not only about novel architectures. It also includes digital education, school administration, community infrastructure, local-language computing, secure payments, and tools that make serious software available where it is usually overlooked.

## What I work with

**AI and ML:** spiking neural networks, recurrent and spectral systems, transformer internals, continual learning, RLHF, LoRA/QLoRA, causal structures, multimodal substrates, and agent orchestration.

**Systems:** C/C++, Python, Rust, TypeScript, Flask, FastAPI, React, Docker, CMake, PostgreSQL, SQLite, native libraries, concurrency, zero-copy I/O, secure protocols, and observability.

**Research direction:** intelligence that can retain knowledge, reason about causes, use resources deliberately, operate through new computational substrates, and serve more than the languages and institutions already represented in technology.
